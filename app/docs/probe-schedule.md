# Adaptive Probe Cadence

Last updated: 2026-08-20

**Current shape:** this schedule runs inside the Coolify Deno container on
Hetzner (paid cron `* * * * *`).  Deno Deploy and Turso are retired.  The
read path is the local SQLite file.  Intervals below are unchanged.

`src/ingestion/probeSchedule.ts` decides **how often** each source is probed.
It does not decide **who** probes — that is the lease in
`src/ingestion/scoutHandoff.ts` (server-preferred, Mac fallback).  The two
compose: the lease elects a prober, this module tells it the interval.

Everything in the module is pure, deterministic, and I/O-free.  Same
`(source, clock, config)` in, same answer out.

---

## 1. The rule being implemented

Owner spec, 2026-08-11, verbatim:

> if X times more likely for filing to be during X timeframe then it is wise to
> have X times more checks during that timeframe in my opinion and I know that
> means less frequent checks during other times but make it proportional to how
> often filings are released during that timeframe based on historical data or
> something ideally while still ensuring some baseline of checks at minimum like
> idk don't let it be more than 4x as often during peaks as lowest times or
> whatever you recommend.

Implemented literally, as arithmetic, in `allocateProbes()`:

| step | what happens |
| --- | --- |
| **a. weight** | `w_i` = measured arrival-event share of window `i` |
| **b. allocate** | `n_i = w_i × B` — proportional, pre-clamp |
| **c. floor** | `n_i ≥ ceil(T_i / maxIntervalSec)` — no window is ever starved |
| **d. ceiling** | `n_i ≤ floor(T_i / peakFloorIntervalSec)`, where `peakFloorIntervalSec = max(minIntervalSec, maxIntervalSec / R)` |
| **e. renormalise** | water-fill the residual over still-unclamped windows in their `w_i` ratio, to a fixpoint |

Step **e** is the one that is easy to skip and fatal to skip: clamping breaks
`Σ n_i = B`, so without redistribution the schedule silently exceeds the cap.
`allocateProbes()` reports both `proportionalProbes` (pre-clamp) and `probes`
(post-clamp) on every window, so the cost of the clamp is visible rather than
buried.

## 3. What this costs — stated plainly

The budget is fixed.  Probing more at peak means probing **less** off-peak.
This is a reallocation, not an increase.

| window (ET, weekday) | today | new | change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| House 08:55–09:40 | 300s | **60s** | **5× faster** |
| House 09:40–12:00 | 300s | 240s | 1.25× faster |
| House 12:00–20:00 | 300s | **613s** | **2.0× slower** |
| House 20:00–08:55 | 1 200s | **1 789s** | **1.5× slower** |
| Weekend, all | 3 600s | 3 600s | unchanged |

**The overnight trough drops from 20 minutes to ~30 minutes, and the mid-day
window from 5 minutes to ~10 minutes.**  That is what pays for 60-second
polling across the 09:00 burst.  Nothing else does.

Weekend cadence is deliberately **not** cut.  The budget is per-**day**, so a
cheaper Saturday cannot fund a denser Monday — cutting it would be pure loss
with no offsetting gain.

A 30-minute weekday / 60-minute weekend floor sits far inside the
`pollSuccessMaxAgeHours = 6h` liveness thresholds in `shared/pipelineHealth.ts`,
so `polling_house` / `polling_senate` do not regress.

## 4. Recommendations the owner asked for

### Tier count: 4 for House and providers, 3 for Senate

Recommended per source from the measured curve, not applied uniformly.

- **House (n = 32 events / 27 days) → 4 tiers.**  The curve genuinely has four
  regimes: a 45-minute burst (78% of events), a late-morning shoulder (9%), a
  low-but-nonzero business tail (13%), and a 13-hour measured zero.  Two tiers
  cannot express both a 45-minute burst and a 13-hour zero without wasting one
  of them.  Five or more would over-fit 32 events.
- **Senate (n = 10 events / 7 days) → 3 tiers.**  12:00–16:00 and 18:00–21:00
  measure 0.75 and 0.67 events/hour — indistinguishable at n = 10.  Splitting
  them would be inventing structure the sample cannot support, so they are one
  merged HIGH tier.
- **Providers → 4 tiers** on the **union** of both chambers' peaks, because one
  provider call covers both chambers.

### Ratio ceiling: R = 30, not the suggested 4

The measured peak:trough **yield** ratio is unbounded — 78% of House arrival
events land in one 45-minute window, and 0 of 32 events landed in the 13
overnight hours across ~381 covered polling-hours.  So the clamp is pure
damage-limitation; it is not tracking anything in the data.

The tail is **already** protected by the absolute 30-minute coverage floor,
which is a stronger and more legible guarantee than a ratio: *every window,
every hour, at least one probe every 30 minutes*.  The ratio ceiling is a
second, redundant guard whose only remaining effect is to cap the peak.

R = 4 with a 30-minute floor forces `peakFloorIntervalSec = 450s`.  Measured
consequence (pinned in the test suite):

| | shipped (R = 30) | R = 4 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| PEAK 08:55–09:40 | **60s** | **450s (7.5m)** |
| HIGH 09:40–12:00 | 240s | 467s |
| MID 12:00–20:00 | 613s | 450s |
| LOW 20:00–08:55 | 1 789s | 716s |

R = 4 makes the 09:00 burst **slower than the 300s schedule it replaces**, and
pours 65 probes/day into the one window with **zero** measured arrivals.  It
converts a latency product into a uniform poller.

R = 30 makes the ratio ceiling coincide exactly with the 60s politeness floor —
so exactly one constraint binds (60s), not two fighting each other.  R stays
configurable; set `PROBE_SCHEDULE_PEAK_TROUGH_RATIO=4` to see the table above
live.

### Floor: 30 minutes, derived not rounded

From the worst acceptable detection delay, not from a round number.  A filing
published at 03:00 ET has no market consequence until the 09:30 ET open;
detecting it by 03:30 leaves six hours of margin, so overnight latency is worth
approximately nothing competitively.  What overnight coverage **is** worth:
off-schedule and corrected publications, the durable per-attempt liveness
receipts the polling health checks depend on, and never having to say "we were
not looking".  30 minutes buys all three for 26 probes/day — 17% of the House
budget.  Zero is never permitted for any window: `probes ≥ 1` is a hard,
tested invariant.

## 5. Where the numbers came from

52-day measurement, 2026-06-21 → 2026-08-11, against the production SQLite
store on the production fleet box (`ssh coolify`, `/data/congress-trade/db.sqlite`,
read-only).

Raw `filings.first_seen_at` is unusable directly: 4 833 of 5 040 rows arrive in
71 bulk-backfill burst-minutes.  Rows were filtered to those (a) filed within
3 days of being seen and (b) first seen ≤30 min after a **logged poll of the
same source** (`ingest_log`, 20 177 poll records).  Arrival counts were then
divided by an **exposure denominator** — covered seconds per ET hour, crediting
each poll-to-poll gap ≤30 min to the hour of its midpoint.

Exposure came out near-flat across all 24 hours (24–32 covered hours per bucket
per chamber), because the existing schedule already polls overnight every 20
minutes.  That flatness is what makes the concentration trustworthy: it is not
a polling artifact.  Direct confirmation — on 2026-07-16 the House was polled at
08:00, 08:06, 08:11, 08:17, 08:23, 08:28, 08:34, 08:39, 08:44, 08:50 and 08:56,
all empty, and 09:02 returned the day's filings.  Across the window we ran
20–140 House polls per day between midnight and 09:00 ET and found nothing.

**Sample size, read before trusting the weights:**

| | value |
| --- | --- |
| House filings passing the filter | 223, in **32 arrival events**, on 27 days |
| Senate filings passing the filter | 21, in **10 arrival events**, on 7 days |
| Rows discarded as backfill/stale | 4 452 |

Weights use **arrival events** ("a new batch appeared"), not filing counts —
two single-day bursts (117 filings at 11:33 on 08-04; 80 filings spread over 25
separate 09:00 hours) distort the filing counts badly.

House first-arrival-of-day quantiles: p25 **09:01**, median **09:02**, p75
**09:06**; 21 of 27 days land in 09:00–09:06, and the latest first-arrival on
any day was 09:19.  Senate first-arrival median **16:05**.

### Two caveats that limit these numbers

1. The window falls **entirely inside the August recess run-up**, which
   suppresses volume.  It locates the **windows** reliably; it is thin for
   permanent **weights**.  Re-measure in a session month.
2. **Senate n = 10 events over 7 days is directional only.**

Both are why every boundary and weight is overridable from config without a
deploy (§7).

### The one interpolation in the table

Hour 09's 25 House events are credited entirely to the 08:55–09:40 PEAK window.
Events were measured at **hour** granularity, so a strict reading cannot place
them inside the hour; the daily first-arrival list — every single one at or
before 09:19 — is what justifies it.  Tightening or widening PEAK is the
highest-leverage boundary in this table, since PEAK saturates the 60s floor and
every extra minute of width costs a probe.  Revisit it first once sub-hour
event counts exist.

### Why there is no per-provider curve

`trade_provider_observations` has 988 rows but only **78 distinct
`first_observed_at` instants** across 7–13 probe-days per provider.
`first_observed_at` is stamped when **our probe ran**, not when the provider
published — so it measures our own scheduler and nothing else.  The provider
tier table is therefore inherited from our own sources (union of both chambers'
peaks), which is honest but not provider-specific.

**Follow-up:** `trade_provider_observations.provider_published_at` already
exists and is essentially unpopulated.  Populating it is the single change that
would make a genuinely provider-specific cadence measurable.

## 6. Two defects found while measuring

### `filing.firstSeenAt` is NULL on the public feed — a sort-order defect

`GET /api/client/v1/feed` with no params returns `ORDER BY t.cursor_seq ASC`
(the `direction` default in `src/delivery/rows.ts`) — **oldest first**.  The oldest 11 820 rows are
`seed_dataset` rows whose `doc_id`s have no row in `filings` at all, so the
`LEFT JOIN` nulls `filedDate`, `firstSeenAt` **and** `sourceUrl` together.

- `?limit=3` → `"filing":{"filedDate":null,"firstSeenAt":null,"sourceUrl":null}`
- `?limit=3&order=desc` → fully populated

The serialization itself is fine (the row mapper in `rows.ts` and the client
serializer in `client/utils.ts` both carry the field).  The defect is
that a public endpoint named `/feed` defaults to oldest-first, silently serving
the seed block with an empty `filing` object to any client that does not pass
`order=desc`.  **Not fixed here** — it belongs to the delivery lane, not this
one.

### The shipped yield bands were guessed, and guessed wrong

`tradeLatency.ts`'s `DISCLOSURE_PUBLISH_YIELD_WEIGHT` carries the comment
*"Politicians almost always file during US business hours"* — an explicit
guess.  Measured against it, the current curve:

- spends `peak` weight on 08:00–09:00 and 11:00–12:00, both **measured zero**
  for the House;
- demotes 16:00–20:00 to `mid`, which is precisely the **Senate's peak**.

`shared/config.ts`'s `DEFAULT_SCHEDULE` is live and unmodified (`poll_config`
row 1 matches DEFAULT exactly, `aggressive_mode=0`).

## 7. Configuration

All keys optional; every one falls back to the shipped default.  Nothing throws
— a malformed value degrades to the default rather than stalling ingestion.

| key | default | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_ENABLED` | `true` | kill switch |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_MIN_INTERVAL_SEC` | `60` | politeness floor; never probe faster |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_MAX_INTERVAL_SEC` | `1800` | **coverage floor**: no window ever slower |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_WEEKEND_MAX_INTERVAL_SEC` | `3600` | coverage floor on weekends |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_PEAK_TROUGH_RATIO` | `30` | R — peak ≤ R × trough |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_RETRY_HEADROOM` | `0.1` | fraction of budget withheld for retries |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_ALLOCATION_EXPONENT` | `1` | 1 = owner's proportional rule |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_HOUSE_BUDGET` | `171` | probes/weekday |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_SENATE_BUDGET` | `171` | probes/weekday |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_PROVIDER_BUDGET` | `240` | nominal runs/weekday |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_EXECUTIVE_BUDGET` | `107` | weekday probes (96 spendable = 15 min flat floor) |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_JSON` | — | full window-table override |

`PROBE_SCHEDULE_JSON` takes a partial override; `ranges` are
`[startMinuteET, endMinuteET)` pairs.  Anything malformed is ignored for that
source only, and the other sources keep their defaults.

```json
{"house":{"weekdayBudget":220,
          "weekday":[{"tier":"peak","ranges":[[535,580]],"events":25},
                     {"tier":"low","ranges":[[0,535],[580,1440]],"events":0}]}}
```

### On the House and Senate budgets

171 probes/weekday is exactly what today's `DEFAULT_SCHEDULE` spends
(11h @ 300s + 5h @ 1200s + 8h @ 1200s).  It is a **self-imposed politeness
budget, not a documented Clerk rate limit** — no such limit is published.  It is
therefore the single knob to raise if the mid-day regression (300s → ~613s)
proves unacceptable in practice.  Provider caps, by contrast, are real and
documented, and the test suite pins the schedule against each one.

### `allocationExponent`

Default `1.0` is the owner's literal proportional rule and is what ships.

For the record: `0.5` would be the expected-detection-latency optimum.
Minimising `Σ p_i · T_i / (2 n_i)` subject to `Σ n_i = B` gives
`n_i ∝ √(p_i · T_i)`, not `n_i ∝ p_i`.  It is exposed as a knob rather than
substituted, because proportional is what was asked for and it is the more
legible rule.

## 8. Integration — WIRED (2026-08-12)

This module shipped standalone in #1760/#1761 and sat with **zero runtime call
sites** — only its own docs and tests referenced it, so the measured cadence was
dead code and the probes kept running on the old fixed interval.  It is now
wired at three entry points.  What follows describes what landed, not a plan.

### 1. `src/ingestion/tradeLatency.ts` — metered latency providers

```ts
// disclosurePublishYieldBand() body:
return probeTierAt('provider', now, { config: schedule });

// disclosurePublishYieldWeight() body:
return probeYieldWeightAt('provider', now, { config: schedule });
```

`probeYieldWeightAt` is normalised so its **time-average over the day is 1.0**
(asserted by test).  `budgetedProbeIntervalSec()` computes
`interval = (secLeft / runsLeft) / weight`, so a mean-1 weight reshapes the
spend across the day **without changing the daily total** — budget-neutral by
construction, which is exactly the property that function needs.  The existing
`Math.max(0.25, weight)` guard can stay; the shipped LOW weight is 0.223, so
that guard will clamp overnight slightly toward more probing, which is fail-safe
and still bounded by the hard remaining-budget check.

`DISCLOSURE_PUBLISH_YIELD_WEIGHT` and the old 08–12/12–16 boundaries are
**retained, not deleted** — `legacyDisclosurePublishYieldBand()` is what
`PROBE_SCHEDULE_ENABLED=0` falls back to.  A kill switch has to land somewhere
known-good, not somewhere untested.

`selectLatencySourceProbe()`'s single `null` return was also split: a lane that
is pacing correctly (`off_cadence`) and a lane that is out of money
(`daily_cap`) used to be the same answer.  Use `evaluateLatencySourceProbe()`
where the reason matters.

### 2. `src/ingestion/watcher.ts` — House / Senate, via `decideSourcePoll()`

`shouldPollNow()` in `src/shared/config.ts` is **unchanged**.  The composition
lives in `watcher.ts` instead, as an exported pure function:

```ts
export function decideSourcePoll(args: {
  source: 'house' | 'senate';
  now: Date;
  cfg: PollConfig;
  lastPollAt: Date | null;
  schedule?: ProbeScheduleConfig;
}): SourcePollDecision
```

It returns the tier, window, interval, elapsed time, reason, and **which
authority decided** — not a bare boolean — because the caller has to log all of
that (§ Observability below).  Two paths, never both:

| condition | authority |
| --- | --- |
| default | `schedule` — the measured windows |
| `PROBE_SCHEDULE_ENABLED=0` | `poll-window` — legacy fixed intervals |
| `cfg.aggressiveMode` | `poll-window` — owner override honoured wholesale |

**The kill switch must not halt polling.**  `shouldProbeNow()` reports
`disabled` with `probe: false`, so the obvious `return shouldProbeNow(...).probe`
would have silently stopped ingestion the moment anyone set the flag.  That is
pinned by a test.

`aggressiveMode` falls back rather than being ignored: under the measured
schedule the 08:55–09:40 peak already runs at 60s (5× faster than aggressive
mode's 180s), but 12:00–20:00 would go from 180s to ~10 min, so honouring the
switch means honouring the legacy path wholesale.  The decision reports the
authority, and the watcher logs it, so it is never a silent downgrade.

### 3. `src/ingestion/scoutHandoff.ts` — the Mac scout, inside the lease

**The lease decides WHO probes; the schedule decides HOW OFTEN.**  They are
**nested, never side by side.**  If they sat side by side, a provider would be
probed by whichever check passed first and the daily-budget guarantee would
evaporate.

Concretely, in both directions:

- **Server** — `runLeasedLatencyProbe()` computes the lease plan first and hands
  `runDisclosureLatencyProbe()` *only* the providers it holds; the cadence is
  then consulted per provider inside that call.  A lane the lease declined is
  never offered to the schedule.
- **Mac scout** — `requestMacProbeLease()` orders its checks
  eligibility → tenure → budget → **cadence** → atomic claim → charge, and
  returns the new `off_cadence` denial.  The cadence gate sits just *before* the
  claim rather than after it for one reason: the claim charges the shared
  ledger, so granting and then discovering it was too soon would spend a call to
  learn nothing.

The Mac cadence clock is its own KV key (`latency-cadence:mac:<provider>`),
deliberately **not** the server's `last_poll:` stamp for the lane — reusing that
would make a scout grant look like a server poll to every liveness check that
reads it, masking a server outage behind scout activity.  It fails **open** on a
read error (unlike the lease, which fails closed), because exclusivity is
already guaranteed by the lease we are nested inside: a lost cadence clock can
cost one extra poll, never a double-poll.

### Observability

`src/ingestion/probeCadenceLog.ts`.  A cadence that silently stops probing looks
exactly like one that is working — both produce nothing in the log — and the
measured schedule makes long silences *normal*, so "quiet" stopped being
evidence of health.  Every decision therefore carries its tier and window:

```
probe cadence: house skip tier=low window=weekday interval=1789s elapsed=240s reason=too-soon authority=schedule
```

That line says "correctly skipped, off-peak".  Nothing at all for hours says the
tick is dead.  Throttled to ~100–200 lines/day: always log a probe and a tier
change, otherwise at most one line per lane per 15 minutes.

### What the wiring actually changed (simulated, weekday)

| | measured | legacy |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| probes in the 09:00–09:06 arrival window | **6** | 1 |
| House probes/day | 149 | 171 |
| House probes 00:00–08:55 ET (measured-zero) | **18** | 35 |
| longest gap between House probes | 1 800s | — |

Six times the coverage at the exact six minutes where 21 of 27 days' filings
land, while spending **fewer** probes overall.  That is the reallocation.

### The full-history sweeps still run

These are what catch anything the tiers miss, and none of them regressed:

- **House** — every poll re-fetches and diffs the **entire yearly bulk index**,
  so any single probe heals a missed filing; the ~30-minute overnight floor is
  enough on its own.  The January prior-year overlap sweep is hourly and
  untouched.
- **Senate** — the **daily deep sweep** widens to the full `SENATE_MAX_LOOKBACK_DAYS`
  window once per UTC day, which needs ≥ 1 Senate poll per day; the floor
  delivers ~48.  Gap catch-up on the last successful poll is untouched.
- **Liveness** — `pipelineHealth`'s `pollSuccessMaxAgeHours` is 3h for both
  chambers; the worst simulated gap is 1 800s (30 min), a 6× margin.

Pinned by `probeWiring.test.ts` as a property (≥ 24 probes/day, no gap ≥ 3h) so
a future retune cannot quietly starve a source below the sweeps.

## 9. Testing

Two suites, testing two different things.

`src/ingestion/__tests__/probeWiring.test.ts` — 17 tests, proving the schedule
is **consulted at runtime**.  It goes through the real entry points
(`decideSourcePoll`, `requestMacProbeLease`, `runLeasedLatencyProbe`) rather
than the pure allocator, against a real migrated SQLite for the lease cases.
This suite exists because the module shipped correct and unread: the numbers
were right and nothing called them.  Covered: a probe inside the peak window is
allowed and reports its tier; the dead window is skipped but never starved past
the 30-minute floor; **a lease-denied lane skips regardless of the schedule, and
is never even offered to it**; `off_cadence` denies a scout renewal without
charging; the kill switch restores the legacy path instead of halting; the daily
full-history sweeps still get enough polls; and the log throttle keeps skips
legible without drowning the log.

`src/ingestion/__tests__/probeSchedule.test.ts` — 41 tests, proving the
allocation is **correct**.

The load-bearing tests there do **not** inspect the allocation table.  They
**simulate the real cron** — one tick per minute across a real ET day, through
`shouldProbeNow` — and count the probes that actually fire.  That is the number
that reaches the provider's rate limiter.

Covered: budget conservation on weekday / weekend / both DST-transition days;
every documented provider cap (UW 240, Quiver 360, FMP RapidAPI 500, FMP free
235/key) including the calls-per-run multiplier; the ratio ceiling actually
binding at R ∈ {2, 4, 8, 30}; no window starved to zero; the 30-minute coverage
floor holding in simulation; DST correctness in both EST and EDT; determinism
and non-mutation of the decision function; yield-weight budget-neutrality;
config parsing, clamping and malformed-input fallback.

Two notes on day length:

- US DST transitions always fall on a **Sunday**, so a 23- or 25-hour day is
  always a **weekend** day.  The weekday profile never sees one.
- The 25-hour fall-back Sunday spends more than the post-headroom allocation.
  That is exactly what the retry headroom absorbs; the **cap** still holds, and
  the test asserts it.

## 10. Deliberate non-goals

**Self-correcting windows** were considered and not built.  Deriving the table
from a rolling query would adapt to recesses and rule changes automatically, but
the read path is a local SQLite file on the Coolify host.  The 2026-08-11
