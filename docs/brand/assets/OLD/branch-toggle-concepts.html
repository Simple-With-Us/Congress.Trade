Congress.Trade · design exploration

Branch toggles: House / Senate / White House

Premise: the Capitol dome is the icon for Congress, and the real building already splits the way the filter does — Senate in the north wing (left, as seen from the Mall), House in the south wing (right). The White House reads differently at tiny sizes because it has a triangular pediment instead of a dome. Letters carry the H/S distinction; the buildings carry the branch distinction.

Every mockup below is live — tap the toggles. Phone frames render in the site's dark theme at 390 px.

Rev 2: concepts 01 + 04 are the chosen responsive pair — 01 (larger, letters fixed) from desktop down to normal phones, 04 as the H·S·P strip when the header can't afford 01. Concepts 02/03 kept below for the record.

01

One Capitol, two wings

Chosen · primary

A single Capitol silhouette where each wing is its own toggle — S lights the left wing, H lights the right — and tapping the dome toggles all of Congress. The White House sits beside it as one toggle. Rev 2: glyphs grew to 30 px, wing blocks are taller, and the letters now sit fully inside them.

02

Three badge chips

Every branch gets an equal-weight chip: dome-with-H, dome-with-S, White House glyph. No grouping, no hidden behavior — three independent switches, same as the shipped chip filter but pictographic.

03

Congress pill with nested H / S

Your suggestion, literally: one pill represents the legislative branch — small Capitol glyph as its emblem, H and S as round mini-toggles inside it. The White House stands alone as the executive toggle. The grouping tells users H and S are siblings before they read anything.

04

Ultra-compact segmented strip — H · S · P

Chosen · compact fallback

The collapse target when the header can't afford concept 01: one segmented control, three letters — H, S, and P for President, keeping the system letter-pure instead of mixing a building glyph into a type row. ~92 px all-in. Below the breakpoint, concept 01 swaps to this; the full labeled chips stay available in the filter sheet.

  • Smallest footprint — survives 320 px alongside search and menu icons.
  • Letter-pure: H · S · P are the same kind of thing, so the row scans as one alphabet, not two icons and a letter.
  • Segmented form already says "these are one filter" without extra chrome.
  • P is the one letter users must learn (it's President, not a chamber); the tooltip and filter sheet spell it out.
  • Mixed on/off states inside one visual object can read as "broken" to some users; needs strong on-state contrast.

To decide before implementation

  1. Dome behavior (concept 01): select-all/clear-all, or purely decorative? I'd make it toggle both wings — it gives "all of Congress" a one-tap gesture and the ≥1-chip-always-on rule still guards against empty states.
  2. Defaults stay as shipped: House + Senate on, White House off — the icons change the presentation, not the separate-by-default contract.
  3. Responsive ladder (decided): desktop and normal phones get concept 01 at the larger scale; when the header genuinely can't afford it, it collapses to the H·S·P strip (concept 04). Tooltips/long-press name the chambers at every size.
  4. State redundancy: on-state = accent stroke + tinted fill + full-opacity glyph; off = dim outline. Never color alone — the fill differences survive grayscale.
  5. First-run hint: one-time tooltip on the White House toggle ("New: Executive branch trades — OGE Form 278-T") doubles as feature announcement for the Trump tracking.

All glyphs are hand-drawn stroke SVGs on the site's tokens (--accent #4f8cff on #15203a) — no icon-font or asset pipeline needed; they inline into dashboardHtml.ts as-is.
